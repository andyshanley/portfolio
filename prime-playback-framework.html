Amazon Prime Video · Playback · 2023

Playback Framework

A north star vision for in-playback experiences across every stream type

Overview

In Q4 2023, I led a north star vision project reimagining the Prime Video playback experience across all stream types (VOD, Live Sports and Linear)

My role
Design Lead
Partners
Design (x2), Research, Product, Engineering
Stakeholders
VP Design, VP Product, VP Sports
Timeframe
2 months to develop the framework
Launch
First shipped with X-Ray for Live Sports, November 2024

Through workshopping, design ideation, close partnerships and user testing, a new framework was created that is internally (and lovingly) referred to as playback's "Up, Middle, Down" framework.

All Living Room playback experiences from 2024/25 onward are set to launch with the new Playback Framework, including Augmented Content for Live Sports (see X-Ray for Live Sports) and X-Ray for VOD. It also heavily influences other device types and categories, including mobile.

"Overall, participants found the new playback framework UI to be simple to understand and navigate."

Research study, November 2023

Context

The situation

X-Ray helps Customers answer quick questions about what they're watching: "Who's that actor on screen?" or "Who's the artist playing the music right now?" On Sports, it answers things like "What's my team's lineup?" and "How much possession has my team had?"

Even though X-Ray is loved by Customers and recognised as a differentiator among streaming services, the experience had barely evolved. VOD hadn't been meaningfully updated in over 12 years, and Sports hadn't received a major update since launch. That led to:

  1. Low discovery and engagement: X-Ray was hard to find, especially for Customers using a remote control.
  2. Inconsistency: VOD paused the video, Live opened a sidebar on the other side of the screen, and neither behaved like the other.
  3. Feature redundancy and a lack of cohesion: Replays, Next Up, Related content, Game Stats, Lineups and more all competed for space in playback.

The opportunity

By this point I'd been working in the playback space at Prime Video for around 12 months. In the absence of UX Research support during 2022, I'd run my own user testing on the existing X-Ray experiences, and I'd built close relationships with key leads across Design, Product and Operations, which gave me a good picture of the Voice of the Customer, both anecdotal and data-led.

The timing helped too. Prime Video's new Living Room experience (which didn't yet include any updates to Playback) was about to ship, and my org was finalising its Operational Planning (OP) cycle for 2024. I felt it was Playback's turn.

During one of my team's offsites in 2023, I presented my data points (with pros and cons) alongside some early concepts.

One of those ideas was a vertical list of cards containing X-Ray content, a more cinematic but still functional experience that lets Customers get quick answers to quick questions by "just clicking up", without needing to pause the video (the VOD experience at the time) or open a sidebar on the other side of the screen (the Live experience).

The hypothesis I started to form was that by previewing content from the Customer's default focus state (the scrubber), and progressively disclosing more as they click up, we'd increase discovery and encourage engagement. It sparked intrigue. After the offsite, my Director and I presented a version to my VP, who started to get bought into where this was heading.

The task

Working with my leadership, we shaped the goal:

Create an in-playback framework that solves for these issues, one that is cohesive across all stream types and is extensible to all current and future features.

My leadership also put me in touch with a couple of key Sports partners who were looking to launch updates to X-Ray for Live Sports for the upcoming European Champions League season. A great fit (and, being a Brit, soccer is my sport!). My next focus was getting the work prioritised into the OP cycle for 2024.

Process

Workshopping the framework

I set up a workshop with other designers and colleagues from Research and Sports Product, many of whom represented key features within the playback space, including Game Stats, Instant Replays, Team Lineups and Standings, Next Up, X-Ray for VOD, Related content carousels and more. Partners joined in person and remotely.

I came armed with a handful of "How might we…" topics (some I'd aligned on in advance with my Sports partners), such as:

  • How best can we converge feature sets, including Replays?
  • How might we make X-Ray more discoverable for those using a remote control?

My ideation method of choice is Idea Speed Dating: early ideas are written down on sticky notes, then people change seats and add to each other's ideas.

We then used Up, Middle and Down as headers in a closed card sort. Each feature was discussed and placed into its respective space.

Design

The "Up, Middle, Down" framework

On a Living Room remote, Customers can perform one of six actions: Up, Down, Left, Right, Back and Select. Up and Down were our options. The scrubber became the horizon line.

Middle (for Controls)

Instant access to contextual controls and accessibility options, like play, pause, skip, audio, subtitles and more

Down (to Discover)

Customers can navigate down to discover other streams related to their current game or title in playback, like other episodes, concurrent games and related titles

Up (to Explore)

Customers can navigate up to explore content that contextually augments what's being seen on screen, allowing the Customer to explore, interact with the title, the game, and their team

Prototype and user testing

Results

The key takeaway was that "overall, participants found the new playback UI for Sports to be simple to understand and navigate."

  • 91.7% task success rate
  • 7 of the 9 participants rated the new navigation as "Very Intuitive"
  • Reaching content using the new Up, Middle, Down framework "just made sense"

Learnings:

  1. Let Customers hold Up/Down to quickly shortcut "getting around the houses"
  2. Reduce cognitive dissonance when entering and exiting a scaled view

Outcome

Framework outcome

The framework for Live Sports ("Project Reign") was approved for OP1 2024.

Impact

  • The new CX gained wider adoption and has been prioritised across other Live Sports and stream types, including VOD, for 2025.
  • All future Living Room playback design work uses the framework CX.
  • A lighter tech lift: the new framework requires 34% fewer components than the existing CX on new platforms.
  • Mobile is also set to use an adapted version of the Playback Framework.
  • A good thing sells itself: adjacent orgs are looking to build their products into Playback using the new framework (current partners include Ads and Retail/Shopping).
